How to buy a Japanese car in Malta

Without getting scammed by the dealers

  1. 1

    Look for the chassis number and ask for the JEVIC certificate

    JEVIC certificates provided by dealers can be forged. Always check the car’s chassis number (usually found in the engine bay or door pillar) against the certificate. Then verify the mileage yourself on the official JEVIC website using the chassis number, and check that the readings are consistent.

  2. 2

    Ask for the auction sheet and export (deregistration) certificate

    Compare the mileage you got from the JEVIC site with the auction sheet, export (deregistration) certificate, dashboard and Maltese logbook issued by Transport Malta. The readings should be consistent and increase logically over time. Any unexplained mismatch is a red flag.

  3. 3

    Ask to have an independent mechanic inspect the car

    Check that the wear on the pedals, steering wheel, seats and gear knob is consistent with the car’s reported mileage. Excessive wear on a supposedly low-mileage car could indicate tampering. Always ask for an independent mechanic to inspect the vehicle before buying.

Why the chassis number is important

Almost every check depends on it. Japanese domestic-market cars often have a short frame number instead of a 17-character VIN: a model code, a dash, and a serial number, for example NHP10-1234567.

Find it in three places and make sure all three match:

  • The Maltese logbook
  • The plate in the engine bay or on the door pillar
  • JEVIC certificate and import documents

If the number on the car does not match the paperwork, stop. Do not continue with the purchase.

Terminology

Get all the documentation you can. A forged paper document can fool you; several independent sources agreeing is much harder to fake.

1

JEVIC certificate and database

JEVIC certifies second-hand cars before export and records condition and mileage in an online database searchable by chassis number. This is your most important check.

Do not trust the paper copy alone. In the fraud cases, dealers used a forged JEVIC certificate showing the fake mileage. Always confirm the figure in the online record yourself.

You need: chassis number, make, model and approximate year. JEVIC certificate number if you have the paper copy.

Official JEVIC e-certificate check ↗
2

Japanese auction sheet

Most exported cars pass through Japanese auctions. The sheet shows mileage, an inspection grade, and a diagram of dents, scratches, rust and repairs. Several online services will find it from the chassis number for a small fee. A good independent cross-check against JEVIC.

You need: chassis number.

3

Export (deregistration) certificate

This official Japanese document records registration history and odometer readings at past inspections. Ask the dealer for a copy and check that the mileage rises in a sensible way over time.

You need: a copy from the dealer.

4

Transport Malta or Maltese Logbook

The Maltese logbook is Transport Malta's registration document for the car. It shows the mileage declared when the car was registered in Malta, which should never be lower than the JEVIC figure. Transport Malta also explains how buyers can check the logbook mileage against the JEVIC database.

You need: Maltese registration number, logbook details and chassis number.

5

UK MOT history (if relevant)

Some "Japanese" cars reach Malta via the UK. The free UK government MOT history shows mileage at every test.

You need: the UK registration number.

Walk away if

  • The seller can't or won't show the JEVIC certificate.
  • Mileage differs between the logbook, JEVIC, the auction sheet and the dashboard.
  • The car shows far more wear than its mileage suggests.
  • The chassis number on the car doesn't match the paperwork.
  • The price is too good. In the reported racket, high-mileage cars were bought cheaply at Japanese auctions and sold in Malta showing low mileage.

Already bought and found a problem?

Affected buyers have been advised to report their case to the Consumers Authority and the police. Keep the logbook, JEVIC record, sales contract and all messages with the seller.
